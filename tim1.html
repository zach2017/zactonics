1 Timothy

How to live when everyone is telling you something different.

Paul writes to a young leader in a city full of confident voices, and tells him how to hold his life and his beliefs together. If you are in college, you are in Timothy's situation almost exactly.

Before you start

The situation behind the letter

Paul is near the end of his life. Timothy is young — probably somewhere between his late twenties and mid-thirties — and Paul has left him in Ephesus to lead a church that is coming apart. People there are teaching things that sound spiritual but are not true. Some are using religion to make money. Some are addicted to speculative arguments that generate heat and produce nothing. Nobody is sure who to listen to.

Paul tells us plainly why he is writing: so that people will know how to conduct themselves in God's household (). This is not a theology paper. It is a letter about behavior, character, and who to trust.

Timothy is not a natural. Paul has to tell him not to be intimidated, to stop being timid, to guard his health, and to let no one look down on him for being young. Whatever picture you have of a first-century church leader, adjust it: this is a nervous young man being handed a mess.

The whole letter hangs on one sentence: the goal of everything Paul is saying is love — out of a clean heart, a good conscience, and a faith that isn't faked.

How to run a session

45–75 minutes. Read the passage out loud together first, in whatever translations people have. Do the observation questions before the discussion questions and don't skip them — the goal is that the text says something to the group that the group didn't already think. Close by naming one thing each person will actually do.

The study

Five sessions

The practical payoff

How do I know what to do and what not to do?

1 Timothy fights two errors at the same time: people who invent rules God never gave, and people who ignore rules God clearly gave. Both feel like conviction from the inside. Here is the framework the letter itself provides.

01

Some things are simply settled

lists behavior that is wrong regardless of context, motive, or how anyone feels about it. Paul's point is that God's law is not a burden to a person who wants to do right — it is a fence around genuine harm to real people. On these questions the issue is not "how do I feel," it's "what did God say." Don't spend group time relitigating them.

02

Most decisions are gray, and God gave a filter instead of a rule

Paul explicitly refuses to add rules where God didn't () — he calls extra man-made restrictions a form of false teaching. For everything else — how you spend a Saturday, what you watch, what you drink, who you date, what job you take — run the decision through these five questions. Tap each one you can honestly answer yes to.

Answer the five questions above about a real decision you're facing.

03

When you still don't know

  • Time reveals things. says some deeds are obvious immediately and others surface later. When it's genuinely unclear, waiting is a legitimate answer, not a failure of faith.
  • Ask someone whose life you would want. is a portrait of a trustworthy person. Use it as a filter for whose advice you take — not the friend who will tell you what you want to hear.
  • Check why you want a rule. Sometimes we want a hard rule so we don't have to keep making a hard choice. Sometimes we insist everything is gray so we don't have to obey. Both are dodges.
  • Watch your life and your doctrine (). If your beliefs are right and your life is falling apart, something in those beliefs isn't actually being believed. If your life looks fine and your doctrine is drifting, the life will follow eventually.

For whoever is leading

Three warnings

  • If the group goes quiet, go back to the observation questions and refuse to move on until someone answers from the text. Silence usually means nobody read it, not that nobody cares.
  • Sessions 2 and 4 will surface real disagreement about gender and church roles. Decide in advance whether you are hosting that conversation or scheduling it separately, so you're not ambushed mid-session.
  • The money session only works if you answer the opening question honestly first. Say your real number.
