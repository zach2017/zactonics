Six sessions  ·  1 Corinthians  ·  For elderships, staff teams & boards

Hold the line
without crushing
the person.

Paul writes to a church that is gifted and fracturing at the same time. He neither lowers the standard nor abandons the people. This study reads his solution alongside the research that explains why it works — cognitive dissonance, team dynamics, and organizational justice.

Read 1 Corinthians Greek interlinear
Format
6 sessions, 75–90 minutes each. Splits into 12 at the Integration break.
Group size
4–12 leaders who share responsibility for the same congregation.
Preparation
Read the passage twice before the session. Leaders read one research source per session.
Produces
A written correction-and-restoration pathway, a standards audit, and dated commitments.
Scripture sets the norm

“You were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified.” Paul's highest standards and his most unconditional grace sit four verses apart.

Research describes the terrain

A person absorbs a hard truth about their behaviour only to the degree that their identity is secured elsewhere. Grace is not the anaesthetic — it is the load-bearing wall.

00 / Before you begin

Four cautions

Read these aloud in session one. Three of them will save you from misusing this material; the fourth will save someone else from being harmed by it.

Group covenant — read aloud before Session 1

We speak candidly about our own church. We do not name people outside this room in ways that would embarrass them. We treat disagreement here as a service to the church, not a threat to it. Nothing said here is repeated as gossip. Each of us names one thing we have done wrong before naming what someone else has done wrong.

Where to read further

Method note

Scripture is normative here; social science is descriptive. Festinger tells you what people do under dissonance. Paul tells you what the church is and what love requires. Never let a research finding overrule a command — but do let it explain why obedience is hard, and why the manner of a correction so often decides its outcome.
