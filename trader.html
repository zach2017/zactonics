Micro Nasdaq Futures — Simple Plan

16px

The one-sentence goal

MNQ pays $2 for every 1 point it moves. To make $20, you need it to move 10 points in your direction — that can happen in one minute.

⚠️ This is a learning page, not financial advice. Futures are risky — you can lose money just as fast as you can make it. Practice on a demo (paper trading) account first.

1. What is MNQ? (middle-school version)

Imagine the 100 biggest tech companies (Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon…) put together into one giant "score" called the Nasdaq-100. The score right now is around 29,400 points. MNQ is a contract that lets you bet on whether that score will go up or down.

$2
You earn or lose $2 every time the score moves 1 point.
0.25
The smallest move (a "tick") is 0.25 points = $0.50.
2 ways
Buy first (go long) if you think it rises. Sell first (go short) if you think it falls. Yes — you can sell something first and buy it back cheaper!

2. The 8:30 → 10:00 → 12:00 CST rhythm

The stock market opens at 8:30 AM CST. The first 90 minutes are usually the wildest. By 10:00 AM CST the market has "picked a mood," and by 12:00 noon CST (lunch) it usually calms down and moves less.

Why 10:00 AM CST matters

Two things collide around then: (1) the morning rush of orders is finishing, and (2) big U.S. economic reports land at 9:00 AM CST (like Consumer Confidence or ISM) after the 7:30 AM CST reports (CPI inflation, jobs). By 10:00 the market has digested the news, so the gap between the open price and the 10:00 price tells you the day's direction. Many days, the noon price continues in that same direction — just slower.

3. The $20-in-1-minute math

One MNQ contract = $2 per point. So:

BUY → SELL (long): Buy at 29,400. Price rises 10 points to 29,410. Sell. 10 × $2 = +$20 ✅
SELL → BUY (short): Sell at 29,400. Price falls 10 points to 29,390. Buy it back. 10 × $2 = +$20 ✅

MNQ often moves 10–30 points in a single minute during the busy morning (8:30–10:30 CST). That's why a 1-minute "in and out" for $20 is possible — but the exact same math works against you: 10 points the wrong way = −$20, plus commissions (about $1–3 per round trip).

6. The simple rulebook

  1. Check the calendar first. CPI, jobs report (7:30 AM CST), and Fed days move prices violently. Beginners should watch, not trade, those mornings.
  2. At 10:00 AM CST, compare to the 8:30 open. Up 30+ points = up mood. Down 30+ = down mood. Within ±30 = no trade.
  3. Trade with the mood. Up mood → buy a small dip. Down mood → sell a small bounce.
  4. $20 target, $20 stop, 1 minute max. Whichever comes first, you're out. No "hoping."
  5. Stop after 2 losses. −$40 plus fees is your daily maximum. Close the laptop.
  6. By noon CST, stop anyway. Lunch chop eats 1-minute traders alive.

MNQ basics — a bit deeper

MNQ trades on the CME exchange almost 23 hours a day, Sunday evening to Friday. It's 1/10 the size of the big NQ contract, which is why beginners use it. You need a futures broker account and "margin" (a deposit, often $50–$2,000+ per contract depending on broker and time of day).

Long = buy first, profit if price rises. Short = sell first, profit if price falls. Every contract you open must eventually be closed with the opposite action.

Official page with contract specs: CME — Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 (MNQ)

Events that hit the 10AM CST price

  • 7:30 AM CST: CPI (inflation), jobs report, PPI, retail sales — the biggest movers. Their shockwave is still rippling at 10AM.
  • 9:00 AM CST: ISM surveys, consumer sentiment, home sales — land right before 10AM, often set the 10AM direction.
  • 1:00 PM CST (Fed days): FOMC rate decision — mornings before it are usually quiet and choppy.
  • Earnings: Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft report after the close; the next morning gaps hard.
  • Surprise headlines: tariffs, geopolitics (e.g., mid-2026 US–Iran tensions) — can reverse a morning instantly.

Full math with fees

Buy 1 MNQ @ 29,400.00
Sell 1 MNQ @ 29,410.00
Move: +10.00 pts × $2 = +$20.00
Commission + fees (round trip): ≈ −$1.50
Net: ≈ +$18.50

Losing side of the coin: a 10-point move against you = −$20.00 − $1.50 = −$21.50. Because fees always subtract, you must win more often than you lose just to break even. That's why the plan filters for "mood" days instead of trading every minute.

Fee schedules vary by broker — check your broker's commission page plus the exchange fees listed on the official CME MNQ page.

Step-by-step: the 10AM $20 plan

  1. 9:55 AM CST: write down the 8:30 open price and the current price.
  2. 10:00 AM: compute the gap. ≥ +30 pts = UP day. ≤ −30 = DOWN day. Otherwise no trade today.
  3. 10:00–10:30: UP day → wait for a 1-minute red candle (a dip), then BUY. DOWN day → wait for a green bounce, then SELL.
  4. Immediately place a bracket order: take-profit +10 pts, stop-loss −10 pts.
  5. 60 seconds later: if neither fired, close at market. Log the result.
  6. Max 3 trades, stop after 2 losses, done by noon CST.

Risk warning — read this twice

Futures use leverage: you control ~$58,000 of index value with a small deposit. Prices can jump many points in one second on news. A "$20 plan" can lose $100+ if you skip the stop-loss or the market gaps through it. Most short-term day traders lose money over time.

  • Practice on a free demo/paper account for at least a month first.
  • Never trade money you need for rent, school, or bills.
  • A stop-loss order is not a guarantee — fast markets can skip past it ("slippage").

📖 Glossary — trading words in plain English

Point
One whole number of the index (29,400 → 29,401). Worth $2 on MNQ.
Tick
The smallest step the price can move: 0.25 points = $0.50.
Long
You bought first, hoping to sell higher. "I'm long" = "I bet on up."
Short
You sold first, hoping to buy back lower. "I'm short" = "I bet on down."
Gap
The difference between two prices — here, the 8:30 open vs the 10:00 AM price.
Stop-loss
An automatic "eject button" order that closes your trade if it loses a set amount.
Take-profit
An automatic order that closes your trade once it earns your target.
Slippage
When your order fills at a worse price than expected because the market moved fast.
Round trip
One complete trade: getting in AND getting out. Fees are charged per round trip.
Paper trading
Practicing with fake money on real prices. Free at most brokers. Start here!

⚖️ Margin & leverage — the deposit and the multiplier

Margin is a security deposit, like putting down $100 to borrow a $58,000 go-kart. With MNQ around 29,400, one contract controls about 29,400 × $2 ≈ $58,800 of index value, but your broker may only require a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars to hold it during the day.

Leverage is why small moves feel huge: a tiny 0.1% index wiggle (≈29 points) is ≈$59 on your one little contract. That's the magic AND the danger — it works identically in both directions.

If your losses eat into your deposit too far, the broker issues a margin call (add money now!) or closes your trade automatically. This is why the rulebook says: small size, always a stop-loss, never money you need.

🎯 Order types — how you actually push the buttons

Market order — "Get me in NOW at whatever price." Fast but can slip a tick or two in busy moments. Use for the 1-minute exit.
Limit order — "Only fill me at 29,495 or better." No slippage, but might never fill if price runs away.
Stop order — "If price hits 29,490, get me out." This is your stop-loss eject button.
Bracket (OCO) order — The pro move for this plan: enter, and the platform automatically attaches BOTH a take-profit (+10 pts) and a stop-loss (−10 pts). Whichever hits first cancels the other. One click, no panic.

Most platforms (paper trading included) support brackets — practice placing one before trading live.
