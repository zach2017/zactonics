01 — Read this before anything else

Three rulebooks apply to you at the same time

You are selling nutritional supplements as an independent distributor. That combination sits inside three separate sets of rules simultaneously, and breaking any one of them can cost you your ad account, your money, or your distributorship.

The rule-makerWhat they controlWorst case if you ignore it
Google Ads Which health products may be advertised, and what you may claim Ads disapproved; repeat offences escalate to permanent account suspension
Your distributor agreement What associates may say, publish, and advertise Distributorship terminated
FTC / FDA Health claims and income claims Legal exposure, personally

None of this means "don't do it." Plenty of people advertise supplements successfully.

It means the compliance work is not optional paperwork you handle later. It is Step 0, and it shapes every decision after it. Everything below is written with those rules built in. Where the strategy differs from a normal small business, it says so and explains why.

02 — Background

What you need to know before the steps make sense

Google Ads is an auction

When someone types "cellular antioxidant supplement" into Google, many businesses could appear. Google runs a lightning-fast auction — faster than a blink — and picks which ads to show. You are bidding against everyone else.

You do not pay when your ad is shown. You pay only when someone clicks and lands on your site. That is cost-per-click . In supplements, expect roughly $1.50–$4.00 per click. It is a competitive category with very large, very well-funded brands in it.

The warning that matters double here

Every AI writing tool will happily produce supplement copy that gets your account suspended. Ask for headlines about an antioxidant and it will cheerfully return "Fights Disease Naturally" and "Boost Your Immune System." Both are policy violations and possibly FTC violations. You must be the compliance filter on top of the AI. The AI does not know your rules. Never paste AI output into a live ad without reading every line against the checklist.

Three kinds of AI, kept separate

Keeping these apart in your head prevents most wasted money.

Type of AIWhat it doesExamples
AI that makes adsWrites the words, makes the picturesGoogle Asset Studio, Claude, ChatGPT, Canva
AI that places adsDecides who sees them, where, at what bidPerformance Max, AI Max, Smart Bidding
AI that watches adsReports what works, shifts moneyGoogle Recommendations, Opteo

Nearly all the value sits in the middle row, and it is already free inside Google Ads. The third row is where beginners waste the most money.

03 — What makes your situation different

Three constraints that shape every decision

CONSTRAINT 01

You probably cannot put tracking on your replicated site

Your company gives you a replicated website — a personal storefront hosted on the company's own domain. It takes orders perfectly well. But you do not own that domain, which means:

  • ✕You cannot verify it in Google Merchant Center
  • ✕You cannot install the Google tag, so you cannot track conversions
  • ✕You cannot install Google Analytics
  • ✕You cannot change the page to match your ad

Without conversion tracking, Google's AI is optimising toward nothing. Pointing ads straight at a replicated site means running blind, permanently.

The fix

Buy a cheap domain you own (about $12/year), build a simple page there that you fully control, run ads to that, and put your replicated-site link on it as the "Shop now" button. You get tracking, speed, and a page that matches your ad. Orders still flow through the company checkout, so your commission structure is unaffected.

CONSTRAINT 02

Your marketing materials likely need approval first

Direct-selling policy documents typically require that sales, marketing, and recruiting materials be company-produced or company-approved before publication, and typically bar associates from using company trademarks in domain names, page names, and social handles.

Do
  • ✔ Buy a domain with no brand name in it — e.g. danshealthcorner.com
  • ✔ Put "Independent USANA Associate" clearly on the site
  • ✔ Send page and ad copy to compliance before launch
Don't
  • ✕ Build anything that looks like an official corporate site
  • ✕ Use the brand in your domain, handle, or page title
  • ✕ Republish corporate photography without written clearance

Do this first, before you spend an afternoon building. Log into your back office, find the current Policies & Procedures and any internet/advertising policy, read the advertising section, and email compliance with exactly what you plan to run. A written yes takes a few days and protects everything you build afterwards.

CONSTRAINT 03

Some supplement advertising is restricted or banned outright

Google's Healthcare and Medicines policy is strict. Some healthcare content cannot be advertised at all, while other content may only be advertised in certain locations by advertisers who have applied and been approved. Where a campaign targets permitted locations with a properly certified domain, the ad is labelled Eligible (limited) and runs only in allowed places.

Under the related unapproved-substances rules, Google does not allow herbal and dietary supplements with active pharmaceutical or dangerous ingredients, products implying they are as effective as prescription drugs, non-government-approved products marketed as safe or effective for preventing, curing, or treating a disease, or products subject to government or regulatory action.

Translation for a mainstream vitamin line

The products themselves are generally advertisable. What gets you banned is the language. The moment your ad or landing page implies a product treats, prevents, or cures anything, you have crossed the line. This catches supplement sellers out constantly — legal products still violate the policy when the marketing makes medical claims, and violations can trigger immediate disapproval or account suspension.

And do not advertise the income opportunity

Ads about earning money from a distributorship run headlong into Google's business-opportunity rules and the FTC's rules on income claims. Advertise products. Recruit through relationships, not paid search.

04 — The complete worked example

Follow this once, exactly, and everything else makes sense

Meet Dan. He is an independent USANA associate. He wants online product orders from people he does not already know. His budget is $15 a day (about $450/month). He has never run an ad in his life. What follows is his entire first week, in order.

STEP 00

Get compliance clearance

2 DAYS OF WAITING · $0

Before building anything, Dan:

  1. Downloads the current Policies & Procedures from his back office and reads the advertising / internet section.
  2. Writes down exactly what he wants to do: "Own domain, not brand-named. Landing page about the daily vitamin line, linking to my replicated site for checkout. Google Search ads on non-branded keywords. No income claims, no health claims."
  3. Emails that to compliance and asks for written approval.
  4. While waiting, does Steps 1 and 2, which need no approval.

He does not skip this. An ad account can be rebuilt in a day. A terminated distributorship cannot.

STEP 01

Build a landing page he actually owns

2 HOURS · ~$12/YEAR + HOSTING

Why this replaces the "Merchant Center" step in generic guides. Merchant Center is a free product catalogue you hand to Google. It requires you to verify and claim the website URL — which Dan cannot do on a domain he does not own. So Merchant Center is off the table until he has his own store. If you do have your own store, see here.

  1. Buys vitalitywithdan.com from a registrar (about $12/year).
  2. Builds one page on Carrd (~$19/year). Squarespace, Wix, or WordPress also work.
  3. Puts on the page: a clear headline about the specific product category he will advertise; what is in it, factually, and the third-party quality certifications the product genuinely carries; price and a large Shop now button pointing to his replicated site; a short "Ask me a question" form; and "Dan Reyes — Independent USANA Associate" in the footer with the standard supplement disclaimer .
  4. Tests it on his phone. It loads in 1.8 seconds. Good.
What he deliberately leaves off

Anything about immunity, disease, energy cures, weight loss results, before-and-after photos, or how much money he makes.

STEP 02

Set up conversion tracking

30 MINUTES · $0 · MOST IMPORTANT STEP

This is the step everyone skips, and it is the most important one in the guide.

Google's AI is a bloodhound. It chases whatever you point it at. Point it at "clicks" and it finds you the world's cheapest, laziest clickers. Point it at "someone clicked through to buy" and it finds you customers.

Dan decides two things count as success: someone clicks his Shop now button, and someone submits his question form. Then:

  1. Google Ads → Goals → Conversions → New conversion action → Website.
  2. Enters his domain and clicks Scan.
  3. Accepts Google's offer to auto-detect the form submission.
  4. For the Shop now button, adds a click-tracking conversion — most site builders let you attach an event to a button without touching code.
  5. Installs the Google tag on his page.
  6. Waits a day and confirms the status reads Recording conversions.
Honest limitation

Dan cannot see the actual purchase on the company checkout, because he does not control that page. His Shop now click is a proxy — a strong signal, not a confirmed sale. He handles this by checking his back office weekly and comparing order counts against Shop now clicks. After a few weeks he knows roughly how many clicks equal one order, and can do the maths from there.

STEP 03

Make ad words and pictures with AI

1 HOUR · $0

Dan opens an AI assistant and — this is the important part — puts the rules in the prompt.

For pictures, he uses Google Asset Studio , free inside Google Ads, to generate clean backgrounds in the square, wide, and tall shapes Google wants. He also photographs the actual product bottles on his kitchen counter with his phone. Real product photos beat AI-generated ones here, because customers want to see the actual thing they will receive.

Do not use corporate photography, logos, or marketing images unless your compliance approval explicitly covers it. That is a trademark and policy issue, separate from Google.

Total spent on creative tools: $0.

STEP 04

Build the first campaign

45 MINUTES · $0 TO SET UP

Dan goes to ads.google.com, creates an account, and clicks the small Switch to Expert Mode link rather than accepting the simplified setup. Expert Mode is not harder — it simply stops hiding the controls.

  1. New Campaign → Sales — not "Website traffic." See the box below.
  2. Campaign type: Search.
  3. Daily budget: $15.
  4. Bidding: Maximize conversions. With no history yet, he sets a Target CPA of $20.
  5. Location: United States. He clicks the small option Presence: people in or regularly in your targeted locations, so he is not paying for clicks from people merely curious about the US.

7 · Negative keywords — how he stops burning money

free · sample · jobs · salary · income · scam · review · pyramid · mlm · compensation plan · distributor · wholesale · coupon code · recall · lawsuit

  1. Pastes in his 8 approved headlines and 4 descriptions.
  2. Adds assets : sitelinks to his product page and FAQ, and short callouts like "Third-Party Tested" and "Ships From US."
  3. Enters payment details. Sets a monthly spending cap in account settings so a mistake cannot drain his card. Clicks Publish.
  4. Expects a disapproval, and does not panic. Health-category ads often come back Eligible (limited) or get flagged on first review. If a specific headline is rejected, he edits that one line and resubmits — he does not delete and rebuild the campaign, and he does not resubmit the same text repeatedly. Repeat violations on identical text are what escalate to account suspension.
Why "Sales," not "Website traffic"?

"Website traffic" tells Google's AI to find people who click. Full stop. You will get a beautiful click count and no customers. "Sales" or "Leads" tells the AI to find people who do something. Pick the goal that matches what you actually want — which is almost never raw traffic.

Why "Search," not "Performance Max"?

Performance Max is Google's all-channel AI campaign and it is genuinely powerful — but hungry. It needs a steady diet of conversion data and spreads a small budget thinly across Search, YouTube, Gmail, Maps, Display, and Discover at once. On $15/day with zero history it usually flounders and you cannot easily see why. Search reaches people actively typing that they want this. Step 8 covers exactly how and when to graduate.

STEP 05

Do nothing for two weeks

14 DAYS · $0 EFFORT

Seriously. Nothing.

Google's bidding AI needs roughly two weeks and 15–30 conversions to finish its learning period . Every budget or bid-target change restarts that clock. The most common beginner mistake is panicking on day three and fiddling — and fiddling costs more than any bad ad ever will.

Dan checks his numbers once on day 7, briefly, and touches nothing.

STEP 06

The 20-minute Monday check-up

FROM WEEK 3 · 20 MIN/WEEK · $0

Four things, every Monday. Nothing else.

  1. Search terms report (Campaigns → Insights and reports → Search terms). This shows the actual phrases people typed, which are never quite your keyword list. Dan finds usana scam, usana distributor income, and cheapest vitamins walmart in there. He adds all three as negatives. This single habit typically saves 10–30% of a beginner's budget.
  2. Recommendations tab. He applies the boring sensible ones (add a missing headline, fix a broken sitelink). He ignores every recommendation that raises his budget or broadens his targeting unless he has decided he wants that. Google's suggestions are useful and, unavoidably, Google's sales pitch. He also ignores any suggestion to add broad-match keywords — in a policy-sensitive category, broad match drags in searches he cannot control.
  3. Asset performance. Google labels each headline Low / Good / Best. He deletes the Low ones and asks his AI assistant — with the same compliance prompt — for replacements.
  4. Cost per conversion, cross-checked against real orders in his back office.

If your numbers are bad

The problem is almost never the ad. In order of likelihood: (1) your landing page is confusing, slow, or asks for too much; (2) you are targeting people who are not ready to buy; (3) your price is not competitive against what they can find elsewhere; (4) the ad. Fix in that order.

STEP 08

Moving from Search to Performance Max

This is the graduation step. Do not do it early — doing it early is the single most expensive mistake in this guide.

First: are you actually ready? All five must be true.

#RequirementWhy it mattersDan at month 3
1Conversion tracking working and trustedPMax is entirely driven by conversion data. Bad data means confidently wasted budget.✔ Yes
2At least 30 conversions in the last 30 daysBelow this the AI has nothing to learn from and never exits learning.✔ 34
3Budget of at least $50–$100/dayPMax splits spend across 6+ channels. $15/day becomes $2.50 per channel — meaningless.✕ At $25. Not ready.
4A deep asset libraryPMax needs 5+ headlines, 5+ descriptions, images in every aspect ratio, ideally video. Thin assets produce poor placements.✔ Yes
5Policy-clean historyPMax pushes ads onto YouTube and Display, where health-claim enforcement is tighter still. Active violations spread.✔ Clean

Dan waits. He grows his Search campaign to $50/day over two more months first. That is the right answer, and it is the one most people will not take.

When you are ready: run them in parallel, never as a swap

  1. Do not pause your Search campaign. This is the number-one error. Search is your proven, profitable engine. PMax is an experiment until it is not.
  2. Add new budget for PMax rather than moving Search's budget. If you cannot afford new budget, you are not ready.
  3. Create it: New Campaign → Sales → Performance Max.
  4. Build one asset group per product theme , not one giant group for everything. Separate the daily vitamin line, the skincare line, and the shake line. Mixing them means the AI shows moisturiser ads to people looking for vitamins.
  5. Add audience signals — these are hints, not hard targeting. Feed it your customer email list, your website visitors, and relevant interest categories. Good signals dramatically speed up learning.
  1. Add negative keywords at campaign level. PMax supports these properly after the 2026 updates. Import the entire negative list you built in Search. Critically, add your Search campaign's winning keywords as negatives in PMax so the two campaigns do not bid against each other and inflate your own costs.
  2. Turn OFF "Final URL expansion" at first. Left on, Google sends traffic to whatever page it thinks is best — including pages you never intended as landing pages. In a compliance-sensitive category, keep the leash short until you trust it.
  3. Set the same Target CPA you are already achieving in Search. Do not set an ambitious one; you will starve the campaign.
  4. Let it run six weeks untouched. PMax learns more slowly than Search.
  5. Check the channel performance report — one of the genuinely useful 2026 additions. It finally shows how much went to Search vs. YouTube vs. Display vs. Shopping. If Display is eating 60% of your budget and producing nothing, you now have the visibility to act.

What success looks like at six weeks

PMax cheaper or equal

Keep both. Gradually add budget to PMax while holding Search steady.

PMax noticeably worse

Check the channel report first — usually one channel is the culprit, and tightening assets or negatives fixes it. Give it one more month.

Still worse after that

Turn it off and go back to Search. That is a perfectly good outcome, not a failure. Many small advertisers do better on Search alone, permanently.

A note on Merchant Center and PMax

The strongest form of Performance Max is the one with a product feed attached — that is what turns on Shopping placements with photos and prices. That requires Merchant Center, which requires your own store on your own domain. If you are still routing checkout through a replicated site, you are running PMax without its best channel. Worth knowing before you expect miracles from it.

About AI Max — a different thing people confuse with PMax

AI Max is not a campaign type. It is a set of AI features you switch on inside an existing Search campaign: search-term matching that goes beyond your keyword list, automatic ad-text generation and landing-page selection, and location-intent targeting. It came out of beta in April 2026. Campaigns using automatically created assets and campaign-level broad match begin auto-upgrading in September 2026, and Dynamic Search Ads start being retired in February 2027.

Should a supplement advertiser turn it on?

Cautiously, and later. Two of its three features — automatic text generation and automatic landing-page selection — mean Google writes ad copy you did not approve and sends people to pages you did not choose. In a category where the wrong sentence gets your account suspended, that is a real risk. If you test it, enable search-term matching first and leave text customisation off until you have watched it for a month. Some of this is being switched on automatically, so check your campaign settings in September rather than assuming nothing changed.

06 — Deep dive

Every option, compared honestly

If you build your own real store

If you eventually build a genuine e-commerce site on your own domain (compliance permitting), Merchant Center opens up and it is genuinely valuable.

What it is: a free digital catalogue. You give Google a list of everything you sell — name, image, price, availability — and Google can show your actual products with photos and prices in search results and the Shopping tab. Free product listings are the underrated part: you get unpaid visibility in Google Shopping.

  1. Go to merchants.google.com, sign in, enter your website.
  2. Verify and claim your website URL. (This is the step that is impossible on a replicated site.)
  3. Add products — easiest first: an official e-commerce plugin (Shopify, WooCommerce, and BigCommerce all have one, and they auto-sync); or Google's automatic website scan; or a manual spreadsheet as a last resort.
  4. Complete shipping and tax settings. This is where most people get suspended — that, or prices in the feed not matching prices on the site. Match them exactly.
  5. Wait 3–5 days for review.

Merchant Center enforces the health policy too. Shopping ads may disallow promotion of any dietary supplement or product that has been subject to government or regulatory action or warning, and disallow products marketed as safe or effective for preventing, curing, or treating a disease. Your product titles and descriptions in the feed must be as clean as your ads.

Best practice: product titles should read like search queries. "USANA CellSentials Daily Vitamin & Mineral Supplement, 112 Tablets, 28-Day Supply" beats "CellSentials." The first 30 characters matter most.

"AI ad managers": what is worth it and what is not

This is where beginners lose the most money.

Madgicx

Often recommended for this, incorrectly. It manages and optimises ads only on Meta — Facebook and Instagram. It can connect Google Ads as a reporting and attribution data source, but it does not run, optimise, or generate ads on that channel. If Google Ads is your plan, it is the wrong tool.

"Ryze AI"

Could not be verified as an established Google Ads product. Never hand ad-account permissions to a tool you cannot verify; that access lets it change your spending.

Opteo

Real, well-reviewed, genuinely Google-Ads-focused. But it starts around $99–$129/month. On a $450/month ad budget that is over 20% of your spend going to software. Not yet.

Use these instead, for free
  • Google's Recommendations tab and optimisation score — the same category of advice, built in, $0.
  • Automated rules (Tools → Rules): "pause any campaign spending over $X," or "email me if cost per conversion exceeds $Y." This is the safety net people pay third parties for.
  • Google Analytics 4 — free; shows what people do after they land, which is where your real problem usually lives.
  • Ads Advisor, Google's in-platform conversational AI assistant, is rolling out. Check whether it is live in your account before paying anyone.

The honest rule: do not buy an optimisation tool until a 10% improvement would pay for it. Below roughly $2,000/month in ad spend, third-party tools cost more than they save.

The free traffic you should build alongside — it matters more than usual here

Paid search is genuinely hard for an individual distributor. You are bidding against enormous supplement brands with real budgets, on a $15/day allowance, in a category where half your best keywords are off-limits. Paid ads should be maybe a third of your effort.

Content that answers real questions

"How to read a supplement label," "what NSF Certified for Sport actually means," "how to tell if a multivitamin is absorbing." Genuinely useful, entirely compliant, and it compounds for years. Use AI to draft; rewrite in your own voice, because AI-flavoured filler ranks badly and reads worse.

Get found by AI assistants

More people now ask Claude, ChatGPT, or Google's AI Overviews for recommendations than scroll results. These systems favour pages with clear, direct answers, real specifics, and mentions on other reputable sites. Write plain FAQs; keep your details consistent everywhere; get listed where you legitimately can.

Email

Every visitor who leaves without buying is gone forever unless you captured their email. A free Mailchimp or Brevo account plus a signup box turns paid traffic into an asset you own — and email is where subscription retention actually gets won.

Google Business Profile

If you have a genuine local presence or offer local wellness consultations. Free, and local intent is far cheaper to capture than national. Claim it here.

Your existing relationships

Unglamorous, but it remains where nearly all of this industry's actual revenue comes from. Ads supplement that; they do not replace it.

07 — AI staff

Hiring the jobs, not the software

A one-person business does not have a marketing department, a copywriter, a bookkeeper, or someone to answer the phone. "AI employee" tools are marketed as replacements for those roles. Most are not. What they genuinely do well is take the repetitive, low-judgement half of a role off your desk.

How to think about it

Do not ask "what AI tool should I buy?" Ask "which hour of my week is both repetitive and low-risk?" Automate that one. Then the next.

Never automate the compliance judgement. In this category the thing you must not delegate is exactly the thing AI is worst at: knowing which sentence crosses a legal line. Keep a human — you — on that.

If you remember nothing else

The 12 rules

  1. Get compliance approval before you build or launch.
  2. Track conversions before you spend a dollar. Everything depends on it.
  3. Never let AI-written copy go live unread. You are the compliance filter.
  4. Advertise products, never the income opportunity.
  5. Pick "Sales" or "Leads" as the goal, not "Website traffic."

  11. Your landing page must load in under 3 seconds on a phone. Most ad money dies here, not in the ad.

  1. Running ads before checking your distributor agreement.
  2. Any health claim, anywhere — ad or landing page.
  3. No conversion tracking, so the AI optimises toward nothing.
  4. Sending paid traffic straight to a replicated site you cannot track or edit.
  5. Starting with Performance Max on a small budget.
  6. Pausing a profitable Search campaign to fund a PMax experiment.
  7. Bidding on brand terms you are not permitted to bid on.
  8. No negative keywords, so you pay for "scam," "review," "jobs," "free samples."
  9. Buying a $49–$99/month AI tool before spending $50 on actual ads.
  10. Judging results by clicks instead of by orders — and, in a subscription business, by retained customers.

More detail

Google splits health advertising into three buckets: allowed, restricted (permitted only in certain countries, often only after you apply for and receive certification), and prohibited (never advertisable, regardless of who you are).

Mainstream vitamins and minerals usually sit in the first two buckets. What pushes an otherwise fine product into "prohibited" territory is almost always the marketing language rather than the product itself.

Enforcement is progressive. A single disapproved ad is routine and harmless. Repeatedly resubmitting the same violating text is what escalates to an account-level suspension, and suspensions are difficult to reverse.

OfficialHealthcare and medicines policy

Every direct-selling company publishes a Policies & Procedures document that forms part of your contract. It is usually available in your associate back office, and it is usually updated more often than people notice.

The sections that matter for advertising are typically titled something like Advertising, Internet and Online Conduct, Trademarks, or Sales and Marketing Materials. Read those in full.

Common restrictions you should expect to find: materials must be company-produced or company-approved; you may not use company trademarks in domain names, page titles, or social handles; you may not bid on company trademarks in paid search; and you may not make health or income claims beyond approved literature.

Get your specific plan approved in writing. A verbal yes from an upline is not protection.

Two things matter here, and they attach to you, not only to the company.

Health claims. A dietary supplement may not be marketed as diagnosing, treating, curing, or preventing disease. That is what separates a supplement from a drug in law. Claims must also be substantiated by competent and reliable scientific evidence — your own experience is not substantiation.

Income claims. The FTC has been consistently active on earnings representations in multi-level marketing. Statements or implications about how much someone could earn are heavily scrutinised, and "results not typical" disclaimers do not fix a misleading impression.

You are charged only when someone clicks your ad and lands on your site. Impressions — times your ad was shown — are free.

What you actually pay is usually less than your maximum bid. Google charges you just enough to beat the next advertiser, and it weights the auction by Ad Rank, which combines your bid with the expected relevance and quality of your ad and landing page. A more relevant ad can outrank a higher bid and pay less per click.

Practical consequence: improving your landing page and your message match is not just a conversion tactic. It literally lowers what you pay per click.

A replicated site is a personal storefront that your company generates and hosts for you on its own domain — typically something like yourname.company.com or company.com/yourname.

They are convenient and they handle checkout, commissions, and product compliance for you. But because you do not own the domain, you cannot verify it with Google, install tracking code, edit the page, or improve its speed.

The workaround: own the top of the funnel, rent the bottom. Your domain hosts the advertisement destination — the page that has to be fast, tracked, and matched to your ad. The replicated site handles only checkout, at the very end.

Three separate things get confused here.

Trademarks in your domain or handle. Company policies typically prohibit this outright. Independent of policy, it also risks trademark infringement, because it implies official affiliation.

Bidding on brand keywords. Google's own policy generally permits advertisers to bid on trademarked terms as keywords. But your contract with your company is a separate agreement, and it very commonly forbids it. Google does not enforce private affiliate or distributor agreements — your company does, by terminating you.

Trademarks in ad text. Google will restrict ads using a trademark in the text if the trademark owner files a valid complaint.

Net advice: do not bid on the brand, do not put it in your domain, and do identify yourself clearly as an independent associate.

Merchant Center is a free catalogue you give to Google. You upload a feed — a structured list of every product with its title, image, price, availability, and identifiers — and Google can then display your actual products, with photos and prices, in search results and the Shopping tab.

Put it in the footer of every page that mentions a product, in text that is legible — not 6-point grey on white.

A conversion is any action you tell Google counts as success. A purchase, a form submission, a phone call, a button click.

Smart Bidding does not optimise toward "good marketing." It optimises toward the specific event you defined, relentlessly. If you define a weak event — a page view, say — it will find people who view pages and leave.

Primary vs secondary. Mark only your genuine money action as a primary conversion. Everything else — newsletter signups, PDF downloads — should be marked secondary so it is reported but not bid on. Beginners commonly mark everything primary and then wonder why the AI chases cheap, worthless actions.

The Google tag is a small snippet of code that must load on every page of your site. Where you paste it depends on your builder:

Asset Studio is built into Google Ads and costs nothing. You describe an image and it generates one, or it adapts an image you upload into the various aspect ratios Google needs (square 1:1, landscape 1.91:1, portrait 4:5).

It uses Google's current image generation model, and the output quality is good enough for backgrounds, abstract concepts, and lifestyle scenes.

Where it is weak: anything that needs to depict your actual product accurately. When you create a Google Ads account, Google steers you toward a simplified experience — historically called "Smart campaigns." It hides most settings and makes decisions for you.

It is not a scam, but it removes exactly the controls that keep a small budget from being wasted: negative keywords, match types, location presence settings, bidding strategy choice, and detailed reporting.

Look for a small link reading Switch to Expert Mode during setup. It is genuinely not harder to use — the walkthrough on this page assumes it. If you have already created an account in the simplified mode, you can switch from the account settings.

Target CPA tells Google roughly what you are willing to pay for one conversion, and Smart Bidding then tries to hit that average.

Two failure modes to avoid:

Setting it too low. If you demand $5 conversions in a market where they cost $25, Google will simply stop bidding and your ads will barely serve. It looks like the campaign is broken; it is actually obeying you.

Changing it constantly. Every change restarts the learning period. Set it, leave it two weeks, then adjust by no more than 20% at a time.

With no history, start with a target that is genuinely acceptable to your economics rather than one that is aspirational, then tighten it once data exists.

This setting hides in the location options and quietly wastes more beginner money than almost anything else.

The default is often "Presence or interest: people in, regularly in, or who've shown interest in your targeted locations." That last clause means someone in another country reading about your city can trigger your ad.

Change it to "Presence: people in or regularly in your targeted locations." Unless you are deliberately targeting travellers or expats, this is almost always what you want.

Broad match — Google shows your ad for anything it considers related. Widest reach, least control. It has become far more aggressive as Google's AI has improved, and on a small budget it burns money on tangents.

Phrase match (written "like this") — the meaning of your keyword must be contained in the search. A sensible default for beginners.

Exact match (written [like this]) — closest control, narrowest reach. Good for your handful of proven winners.

In a policy-sensitive category, tighter match types are also a compliance control: they reduce the chance your ad appears next to a search you would never have chosen to appear for.

Assets are the extra pieces attached to your ad: sitelinks to specific pages, callouts (short phrases like "Third-Party Tested"), structured snippets, phone numbers, prices, and images.

They are free. They make your ad physically larger on the page, which raises click-through rate, and Google factors expected asset performance into Ad Rank — so they can lower your cost per click too.

There is no good reason to skip them. Add at least four sitelinks and four callouts to every campaign on day one.

Do this, in order:

1. Read the specific policy cited. Google names it. Do not guess.

2. Find the offending element. It may be in the headline, the description, the image, or — very often — on the landing page rather than in the ad at all.

3. Fix and resubmit once. Editing an ad automatically resubmits it for review.

4. If you genuinely believe it is a false positive, request a manual review rather than resubmitting repeatedly.

What not to do: do not delete and recreate the same ad hoping for a different reviewer, and do not submit the same text again unchanged. Repeat violations on identical content are what escalate from ad-level disapproval to account-level suspension.

When you launch a campaign, or make a significant change to budget, bidding strategy, or conversion setup, Smart Bidding enters a learning phase. It is deliberately exploring rather than exploiting — testing bids and audiences to build a model.

Performance during learning is unreliable and usually worse than what follows. It typically lasts about a week or two, and needs roughly 15–30 conversions to complete.

The trap: someone sees poor day-4 numbers, changes the budget, restarts learning, sees poor numbers again, changes something else. The campaign never leaves the learning phase and never performs. Discipline here is worth more than any optimisation.

Your keywords are what you asked to appear for. Search terms are what people actually typed. They are never the same list, and the gap is where your money leaks.

Find it at Campaigns → Insights and reports → Search terms. Sort by cost, descending, so the expensive mistakes appear first.

For each irrelevant term, add a negative keyword. Use a phrase-match negative when a whole concept is wrong ("jobs"), and an exact-match negative when only one specific query is wrong.

Doing this once a week for the first three months is the highest-return twenty minutes in the whole process.

If you judge ads on the first order alone, almost nothing looks profitable. A $50 acquisition cost against a $130 order with product cost and commission structure taken out can look marginal or negative.

In a subscription business, the first order is not the product. The relationship is. If the average customer stays six months at $130, that is roughly $780 of revenue from a $50 acquisition.

The number that decides everything is retention, and you will not know it for three months. So track it from day one: of the customers acquired in month 1, how many are still ordering in month 3? In month 6?

Until you know that, be conservative about scaling. After you know it, you may find you can afford to pay considerably more per customer than competitors who only look at first-order profit.

An asset group is a themed bundle: headlines, descriptions, images, videos, and a landing page URL, all about one thing. It is the PMax equivalent of an ad group.

The common mistake is one giant asset group containing everything you sell. The AI then mixes and matches across unrelated products and shows skincare creative to someone researching vitamins.

One asset group per product theme. Each with its own images, its own copy, and its own landing page. If you sell three distinct lines, that is three asset groups minimum.

Audience signals are hints, not restrictions. This trips people up constantly.

When you add a signal — your customer list, your website visitors, an in-market segment — you are telling Google "people like this are good starting points." Google will start there and then expand outward as it learns. It will not restrict itself to that audience.

If you need genuine hard restriction, PMax is the wrong campaign type. What signals do accomplish is dramatically shortening the learning period, which on a limited budget matters a great deal.

The strongest signal available to you is your own customer list, uploaded as a Customer Match audience — real buyers beat any interest category Google can infer.

AI Max is not a campaign type — this is the single most common misunderstanding. It is a settings layer you enable inside an existing Search campaign. Three components:

Search term matching — reaches relevant queries beyond your keyword list using keywordless technology.

Text customisation and final URL expansion — generates ad copy dynamically and picks the landing page it thinks fits best.

Locations of interest — targeting based on geographic intent in the query.

A growing share of product research now happens inside AI assistants and AI-generated search summaries rather than in a list of blue links. Being the page an assistant draws on is becoming its own channel.

What appears to help, based on how these systems work:

  • Answer the question directly, early. A page that states its answer in the first two sentences is far easier to cite than one that builds to a conclusion.
  • Be specific and factual. Numbers, named certifications, and concrete comparisons are quotable. Marketing adjectives are not.

What it genuinely does well: volume and variation. Twenty headline options in ten seconds, each within a character limit. Rewriting one paragraph in five registers. Turning an article into ten social posts. Adapting copy to a new format without losing meaning.

What it does badly: knowing what is true about your product, knowing what is legally permissible, and sounding like a specific human being. Its default register is smooth, generic, and slightly overexcited.
