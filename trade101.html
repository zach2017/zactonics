      Section 01

      Futures basics: what you're actually trading

      Stock index futures

      A futures contract is a bet on which way a whole stock index (like the S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100) will move. You never buy any actual stocks — you just trade the direction, almost 23 hours a day. Common symbols: ES, NQ, YM, RTY — and their smaller “micro” versions MES, MNQ, MYM, M2K.

      Micro futures — the beginner's entry point

      Micro contracts are 1/10th the size of the regular ones. If the market moves 1 point, an MES trade changes by $5 instead of $50. Smaller size means smaller mistakes while you learn. Every beginner should start here.

      Options on futures

      An option is like paying a small fee for the chance to profit if the market moves your way. If you’re wrong, the most you can lose is that fee. That known, limited loss makes options a safer tool around big news days like Fed meetings.

      Leverage cuts both ways

      Futures let you control a lot of money with a little money. That means a small market move makes a big change in your account — in both directions. Protecting yourself from losses isn’t extra credit. It is the job.

      Section 02

      NinjaTrader setup, step by step

      NinjaTrader is free to download and practice on. “SIM” means simulator: fake money, realistic platform. Do every step below with fake money first — no exceptions.

      1. 01

        Download & install

        Go to ninjatrader.com and download the free desktop platform (it runs on Windows; Mac users use a virtual machine or an online server). Make a free account — that unlocks the practice simulator with free data.

      2. 02

        Connect a data feed

        In the app: Control Center → Connections. Connect to the free practice data — this streams prices into your charts. (Serious traders later pay for faster feeds like Rithmic or CQG, but you don’t need that yet.)

      3. 03

        Open a chart & learn the DOM

        New → Chart → type MES 09-26. Also open the SuperDOM — the order window. Practice “bracket orders”: one click that sets your entry, your stop-loss (your maximum loss), and your target (your goal) all together.

      4. 04

        Set ATM strategies (automatic risk)

        An ATM template automatically attaches a stop-loss and a profit target to every trade you make — for example, risk 10 ticks to try to make 15. It’s your seatbelt. Never trade without it.

      5. 05

        Build your first automated strategy — no code

        Control Center → New → Strategy Builder. This tool lets you build a trading robot using menus and clicks — no coding. You pick the rules (like “buy when the fast average crosses above the slow average”) and it writes the actual code for you behind the scenes, which you can open and edit later if you want.

      6. 06

        Backtest in Strategy Analyzer

        Backtesting asks: “If my robot had run for the last two years, would it have made or lost money?” Use New → Strategy Analyzer, and include fees in the test — they matter. Then test again on data the robot has never seen, to make sure it didn’t just memorize the past.

      7. 07

        Run live in SIM for weeks, then go micro

        Turn the robot on with fake money (right-click the chart → Strategies) and let it run for weeks. Only if it stays good do you try real money — starting with exactly ONE micro contract.

      Section 03

      Automation strategies that traders actually run

      These are the robot styles real traders use, explained simply. Tap any card for more detail and how it can fail.

      TREND

      Opening Range Breakout (ORB)

      Watch the first 15–30 minutes of the day and mark the high and low. If price breaks above that early high, buy; if it breaks below the low, sell. Simple rules — easy to turn into a robot.

      TREND

      EMA crossover trend following

      Draw a fast average and a slow average of the price. When the fast one crosses above the slow one, the market may be trending up — buy. When it crosses back down — sell. The classic first robot to build.

      REVERSION

      VWAP mean reversion

      Some days price stretches too far from its average, then snaps back like a rubber band. This robot bets on the snap-back. It works on calm days and loses on strong trend days — so it needs a filter that turns it off.

      HYBRID

      Partial automation

      You pick the trade; the computer presses the buttons — instant entry, exact stop-loss, correct size. You keep the thinking; the computer removes the fumbling and the fear. This mix is what most serious home traders actually run.

      ML

      AI trade filter

      Keep your simple robot, but add an AI “grader” that scores every trade idea. The robot only takes trades with a high grade and skips the weak ones. The AI improves a working plan — it doesn’t invent one from nothing.

      SEARCH

      AI Generate (built into NinjaTrader)

      NinjaTrader’s built-in experiment: it automatically mixes and tests thousands of rule combinations and shows you the best ones, with math checks to avoid fooling itself. Treat its results as ideas to test — not buried treasure.

      Section 04

      AI agents: planning trades around events & conditions

      A bot follows one fixed rule (“if this number drops, buy”). An agent is smarter: it can read the news, check the calendar, and adjust the plan — and then it must follow strict safety rules before any trade happens. Three layers make that work:

      LAYER 1

      Research & reasoning (LLM)

      An AI model (like Claude) reads the news, the economic calendar, earnings dates, and Fed statements, then writes a simple plan: what’s happening this week and what to watch out for.

      LAYER 2

      Decision framework

      Hard rules act like a parent signing a permission slip: maximum trade size, daily loss limit, “no trades during big news,” allowed markets and hours. The AI can suggest anything — the rules decide what’s actually allowed.

      LAYER 3

      Execution

      Software that actually presses the buttons — placing and closing orders through NinjaTrader or a broker’s connection. It only ever does what layers 1 and 2 approved.

      An event-driven agent workflow (options on futures example)

      DAILY LOOP (agent runs pre-market, e.g. 07:30 ET)
1. FETCH   economic calendar → next 5 days (CPI, FOMC, NFP, earnings)
2. ASSESS  regime: trend vs range, realized vs implied volatility, VIX level
3. PLAN    per event:
           - "CPI Thursday 08:30: go flat futures by 08:15;
              defined-risk options only (long straddle or skip)"
           - "Quiet Tuesday, range regime: enable VWAP reversion on MES,
              max 2 contracts, daily loss limit $150"
4. PROPOSE orders → human approves (or auto within strict limits)
5. EXECUTE via platform API; log every decision + reasoning
6. REVIEW  end of day: agent writes a journal entry; human reads it

      Why events matter for options

      Big scheduled news — Fed meetings, inflation reports, giant tech earnings — makes markets jump. An AI agent never forgets a date. It can list every event, look up how big the moves usually are, and plan ahead: either stay out, or use an option where the most you can lose is known in advance.

      Open-source agent frameworks

      Free projects like TradingAgents, FinRL, FinRobot, and ai-hedge-fund let you watch teams of AI “analysts” debate each trade decision. They are great for learning and practicing with fake money — they are not money machines.

      Section 05

      What's actually working: examples & lessons from 2025–2026

      Honest headline: results are mixed. The real wins come from computers enforcing good habits — not from predicting the future.

      Discipline enforcement beats prediction

      Bloomberg-reported retail agent deployments in 2026 found results "mixed but directionally significant" — one trader's agent refused to chase Nvidia momentum after earnings, avoiding a large weekly loss. The win wasn't a clever forecast; it was the machine enforcing a rule the human would have broken.

      Agents as trade journals: the highest-ROI use for beginners

      AI journal agents (e.g., TradeZella's Zella AI) analyze your own trade history and surface patterns like "your win rate drops from 58% to 34% in the first 30 minutes of the session." For discretionary traders this analysis layer often improves results more than any execution bot, because the bottleneck is self-knowledge, not speed.

      Multi-agent research systems on prediction markets

      On event/prediction markets, autonomous agents (e.g., Olas's Polystrat on Polymarket) executed thousands of trades in a month with some standout individual returns — while data shows most human traders on those venues lose. The takeaway for futures/options traders: 24/7 coverage plus emotionless rule-following is a real structural edge; headline return numbers are cherry-picked.

      The cautionary side

      The same 2026 reporting is full of losses: DCA bots buying "oversold" dips blind to a hawkish Fed transcript released minutes earlier, marketplace strategies overfit to a two-week window, and traders who overrode their own agents on gut feel. Vendor-published agent returns (100%+ annualized claims) are marketing until verified on your own paper account.

      Section 06

      Best practices — the non-negotiables

      1 · Simulate first, always

      Practice with fake money for weeks. Then trade exactly one micro contract. A good backtest alone is never permission to use real money.

      2 · Risk ≤ 1–2% per trade

      Never risk more than 1–2% of your account on one trade. Pick your stop-loss first, then figure out how many contracts fit. Set a daily loss limit the platform enforces — not your willpower.

      3 · Guard against overfitting

      Don’t let your robot “teach the test.” A robot tuned to look perfect on old data usually fails on new data. Always test on data it has never seen, and prefer fewer, simpler rules.

      4 · Model costs & slippage

      Fees and slippage (getting a slightly worse price than you wanted) quietly eat profits. Include them in every test, or your results are make-believe.

      5 · LLM proposes, rules dispose

      Never let an AI place trades without limits. The AI suggests; strict rules approve. When starting out, a human should say yes to every single trade.

      6 · Plan around events

      Know the news calendar. On big report days (Fed, inflation, jobs), beginners should be out of the market — or only in trades where the maximum loss is known. Anything else is gambling.

      7 · Production infrastructure

      Robots need reliable computers. If your PC crashes while a trade is open, that’s real money at risk. Serious traders run robots on an always-on rented server (a VPS).

      8 · Log everything, review weekly

      Write everything down: every trade, every reason. Review it weekly. If results get worse than your tests predicted, stop the robot and investigate — don’t fiddle with it while it’s live.

      Stock index futures — details

      Index futures are standardized contracts on CME Group exchanges. Key specs to know: tick size (smallest price move), tick value ($ per tick), contract months (quarterly: Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, e.g., "ES 09-26"), and margin (intraday vs overnight). Futures trade nearly 23h/day Sunday evening through Friday, with a daily maintenance break.

      Micro E-mini contracts

      Launched by CME in 2019, micros are 1/10th of E-minis: MES ≈ $5/point ($1.25/tick), MNQ ≈ $2/point ($0.50/tick). Typical intraday margins at futures brokers run roughly $40–$200 per micro contract (varies by broker and volatility). A beginner risking $20–$40 per trade on one MES contract can survive the learning curve that would destroy an account trading full-size ES.

      Options on futures — the event tool

      Buying a call/put on ES defines your worst case: the premium. Around binary events, common defined-risk structures include long straddles/strangles (betting on a big move either way), vertical spreads (directional with capped risk), and iron condors (betting the move stays inside a range — riskier around events). CME also lists Micro E-mini options for smaller accounts. Selling naked options into events is where accounts die; beginners should stick to long or spread structures.

      Risk rules of thumb

      • Risk only 1% (2% max) of your account on any single trade. Choose the stop first; the position size comes from it.
      • Set a daily loss limit (about 2–3 losing trades’ worth). When it’s hit, the platform locks you out for the day — automatically.
      • Never add more contracts to a losing trade hoping it comes back. That’s how accounts blow up.
      • Beginners: be out of the market during big news (Fed, inflation, jobs reports), or only in trades where your maximum loss is known.
      • Losing streaks are normal: even a coin-flip system will lose 6+ times in a row sometimes. Trade small enough that a streak like that is boring, not scary.

      Opening Range Breakout

      Logic: record the high/low of the first 15–30 minutes after the 9:30 ET equity open. Buy a break above the high (sell a break below the low) with a volume filter, stop at the opposite side or mid-range, target 1–2× the range. Common failure: choppy days generate whipsaw double-breaks — many implementations allow only one trade per direction per day and skip days with unusually small ranges. Fully expressible in Strategy Builder with time filters.

      EMA crossover trend following

      Logic: fast EMA crossing above slow EMA = long bias; crossing below = short/exit. Works because index futures spend meaningful time in institutional-flow-driven trends — and bleeds money in ranges, so pair it with a regime filter (e.g., ADX > 20, or price distance from VWAP) and a trailing stop. It's the ideal first Strategy Builder + Strategy Analyzer project: simple enough to understand every trade in the backtest.

      VWAP mean reversion

      Logic: when price stretches 1.5–2 standard-deviation bands away from VWAP without new information, fade back toward VWAP with a tight stop. Its edge is regime-dependent — profitable in balanced/range sessions, dangerous on trend days (you're fading a freight train). This is exactly where an AI condition layer helps: classify today's regime pre-market (event calendar, overnight range, gap size) and only arm the strategy on range-probable days.

      Partial automation (human-in-the-loop)

      Some judgments — "is this retest holding?", "does this pullback look healthy?" — are genuinely hard to codify. The pragmatic 2026 pattern: the trader identifies the level or setup; automation then handles what machines do better — instant entry execution, exact stop/target placement via ATM templates, position sizing math, trailing logic, and the daily loss lockout. You keep the judgment, delete the execution errors and the emotion.

      ML trade filter — the practical AI pattern

      Workflow: (1) collect historical bars + indicator values + your strategy's signals; (2) train a classifier (gradient boosting or a small neural net) to predict whether each signal would have hit target before stop; (3) export the model (ONNX, or .NET-native via Accord.NET/ML.NET); (4) load it inside a NinjaScript strategy; (5) only take signals scored above a probability threshold. This enhances a working edge rather than trying to conjure one — the approach with the best track record for retail ML.

      Libraries: Accord.NET · ML.NET · ONNX Runtime

      AI Generate (Strategy Analyzer)

      NinjaTrader's built-in experimental optimizer combines up to 73 default indicators, 25 candlestick patterns, and single-series custom indicators, using a genetic algorithm to search entry/exit combinations. To guard against curve-fitting it validates each generation with a Monte Carlo simulation at a 95% confidence interval. Treat its output as idea generation — you still walk-forward test and sim-trade anything it finds before believing it.

      Event playbook

      • FOMC (8×/year, 14:00 ET): flat futures into the statement; expect violent two-way moves through the press conference. Defined-risk options or nothing.
      • CPI / PPI (monthly, 08:30 ET): pre-market release gaps index futures instantly. Same rule.
      • Nonfarm Payrolls (first Friday, 08:30 ET): similar profile to CPI.
      • Mega-cap earnings weeks: NQ/MNQ volatility clusters around NVDA/AAPL/MSFT reports after the close.

      An agent's job: pull the calendar weekly, compute the instrument's average historical move per event type, compare to what the options market is pricing, and pre-write the plan (flat / straddle / reduce size) so the decision is made before adrenaline exists.
